# 446MHz-Tape-Measure-Yagi
A rugged, portable, and high-gain 3-element Yagi-Uda antenna designed for the 70cm amateur radio band (446.000 MHz). It utilizes standard steel tape measure blades for the elements and custom 3D-printed PETG brackets for structural mounting.

This antenna was designed specifically for Amateur Radio Direction Finding (ARDF), commonly known as fox hunting, as well as portable simplex operations. Tape measure elements offer a unique advantage in the field: they are highly directional for tracking signals, but they can safely fold and snap back into place when walking through heavy brush or trees. It provides excellent RF performance for locating hidden transmitters without the fragility of solid aluminum or copper tubing.

Build Guide

Element Cutting: Cut the tape measure blades to length. The reflector is 347 mm, the director is 286 mm, and the driven element consists of two separate 153 mm halves.

Boom Spacing: Mount the printed PETG blocks to your central boom. The center-to-center distance from the reflector to the driven element is 116 mm. The distance from the driven element to the director is 92 mm.

Feed Gap Alignment: Ensure the two halves of the driven element are mounted with exactly an 8 mm air gap between them at the center.

Surface Preparation: Use sandpaper or a grinding tool to completely remove the paint and clear coat from the inner feed-point edges of the driven elements to expose bare steel.

Soldering the Feed: Apply flux and pre-tin the bare steel using a high-wattage iron. Strip your RG-58 coax, twist the copper shield into a pigtail, and pre-tin both the shield and center core. Solder the center conductor to one side of the 8 mm gap and the shielded pigtail to the other. Ensure they do not physically bridge the gap.
